# GAPS Product Vision

## Vision Statement

For independent developers and small studios who struggle to maintain consistent AI-generated game assets, GAPS is a version-controlled game-asset production system that transforms approved specifications and visual references into traceable build requests, validated outputs, and production records.

Unlike ad hoc prompt libraries, GAPS treats the repository as the source of truth and prevents unapproved variation from silently entering a game.

## Problem

AI image generation often introduces style drift, palette drift, perspective changes, incorrect transparency, inconsistent proportions, uncontrolled redesign, missing metadata, irreproducible prompts, and broken animation continuity.

These problems consume time, energy, and money and can prevent small teams from shipping.

## Target Users

- independent game developers
- small game studios
- technical artists
- AI-assisted asset creators
- Unity developers
- educators teaching production pipelines

## Product Value

GAPS provides:

- a canonical GameArtBible
- structured asset specifications
- compiler-generated build instructions
- reference-image roles
- asset identity locking
- validation and approval gates
- version history
- production reports
- Unity-oriented export controls

## Business Goal

Create a sustainable stream of income through commercial GAPS releases, paid starter templates, Unity packages, consulting, validated asset packs, games produced with GAPS, or training material.

## Product Principles

1. The repository is the source of truth.
2. Prompts are generated build artifacts.
3. AI may propose; the Project Owner approves.
4. Gold Masters are never overwritten.
5. Invalid assets are rejected before production.
6. Every revision is traceable.
7. Automation must reduce workload.
8. Every sprint must create something executable, demonstrable, or usable.

## Success Measures

GAPS succeeds when:

- a new asset can be specified without rewriting a prompt;
- invalid output is detected automatically;
- another developer can use the system without this conversation;
- one complete asset reaches Unity through the controlled pipeline;
- external users can install and evaluate GAPS;
- the system contributes to released products or direct revenue.
